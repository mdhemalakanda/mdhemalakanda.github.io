    Settings Reference

    Account tab

    Your FedEx account number, the address your parcels ship from, and the sandbox/production switch.

    Watch the video walkthrough, then follow the written steps below. WordPress Admin → FedEx Shipping → Settings, then the Account tab. This tab identifies who ships: the FedEx account that gets billed, the contact printed on labels, and the address every parcel starts from. It also holds the Environment switch between sandbox and production.
    Labels ship from this address. Return labels use the same address as the recipient — it is where your customers send returns to. The street lines of the address are entered separately on the Labeling tab.
    The FedEx Shipping settings page, Account tab. One Save Changes button at the bottom of the page saves all five tabs together — you cannot save a single tab on its own. Saving also refreshes stored shipping prices, so the next quote is fresh. All settings are saved with your WordPress site; there are no files or databases for you to touch.

    ​
    Account Number

    A text field for your 9-digit FedEx shipping account.
    Account Number
    What it meansThe FedEx account that gets billed for the labels you create.
    Why it mattersThe plugin uses it nearly everywhere: checkout prices, labels, pickups, returns, and shipment cancellation.
    When to change itWhen you open a new FedEx account, or when you move between testing and live shipping (see the mistake below).
    DefaultEmpty until you enter a value and save.
    After you saveThe next quote, label, or pickup uses the new account.
    Common mistakesEntering your production account number while still testing in sandbox — FedEx’s test servers only accept the test account number. Leaving it empty, so quotes and labels have no account to bill.

    ​
    Person Name

    A text field.
    Person Name
    What it meansThe contact name printed on your labels as the sender.
    Why it mattersIt also appears on return labels as the recipient — the name your customers send returns to.
    When to change itWhen the responsible person or business name changes.
    DefaultEmpty until you enter a value and save.
    After you saveThe next label or pickup shows the new name.
    Common mistakesLeaving it empty, so labels have no sender name.

    ​
    Phone Number

    A text field.
    Phone Number
    What it meansThe contact phone number for the shipper.
    Why it mattersFedEx prints it on labels and uses it for pickup contact details. Return labels show it as the recipient’s phone.
    When to change itWhen your store’s phone number changes.
    DefaultEmpty until you enter a value and save.
    After you saveThe next label or pickup shows the new number.
    Common mistakesLeaving it empty — return labels then print a placeholder phone number.

    ​
    Postal Code

    A text field.
    Postal Code
    What it meansThe postcode your parcels ship from.
    Why it mattersTogether with the Country below, it is the starting point of every price quote. A wrong postcode means wrong prices. It is also printed on labels and used for pickups and return labels.
    When to change itWhen your store moves.
    DefaultEmpty until you enter a value and save.
    After you saveThe next quote and the next label use the new postcode.
    Common mistakesEntering a postcode in a format your country does not use — match the format FedEx expects for the selected country.

    ​
    Country

    A dropdown listing every country WooCommerce knows.
    Country
    What it meansThe country your parcels ship from.
    Why it mattersIt decides whether a shipment is domestic or international, which changes the services and prices FedEx offers. It is also used on labels, pickups, and return labels.
    When to change itOnly when your store moves to another country.
    DefaultNone saved — the first entry in the dropdown shows until you choose.
    After you saveThe next quote and the next label use the new country.
    Common mistakesLeaving the first entry selected without checking it is really your country.

    ​
    City

    A text field.
    City
    What it meansThe city your parcels ship from.
    Why it mattersIt appears on labels, pickups, and return labels.
    When to change itWhen your store moves.
    DefaultEmpty until you enter a value and save.
    After you saveThe next label or pickup shows the new city.
    Common mistakesExpecting it to change checkout prices — quotes are calculated from the Postal Code and Country, not the city.

    State Or Province Code

    A dropdown. The on-screen placeholder reads “Select a state…”.
    State Or Province Code
    What it meansYour ship-from state or province, for example CA or NY.
    Why it mattersIt appears on labels, pickups, and return labels.
    When to change itWhen your store moves.
    DefaultEmpty until you choose.
    After you saveThe next label or pickup shows the new state.
    Common mistakesLooking for your state and not finding it: the dropdown only lists the states and provinces WooCommerce knows for the selected country. If your country has no list, leave the field empty.

    Environment

    A dropdown with two choices: Sandbox and Production. The on-screen help text says: “Sandbox issues test labels only; production creates real shipments with real charges.”
    Environment
    What it meansSandbox is test mode: test labels only, nothing real ships, nothing is charged. Production is live: real shipments with real charges.
    Why it mattersIt points the plugin — and your credentials — at either FedEx’s test servers or the real ones. A badge at the top of the Settings page shows the current mode: orange Production or purple Sandbox.
    When to change itSwitch to Production only when you are going live. See Going live.
    DefaultThere is no saved default. A fresh install behaves as sandbox until you deliberately switch.
    After you saveThe badge updates and the plugin talks to the other FedEx server from its next request.
    Common mistakesUsing sandbox keys with Production mode, or production keys with Sandbox mode. The two are not interchangeable — every call fails authentication if they are mixed.
    The dropdown selects the FedEx server for all calls the plugin makes:
    EnvironmentFedEx server
    Sandboxhttps://apis-sandbox.fedex.com
    Productionhttps://apis.fedex.com
    Switching to Production turns checkout label creation into real, paid shipments — see Instant labels. If credentials and environment do not match, every call fails authentication, and the checkout log (source teamzlab_fedex_shippnig under WooCommerce → Status → Logs — the source name is spelled that way in the code) shows the exact error.

    Where this address is used

    The Account tab address is the starting point for:
    • every checkout rate request,
    • the rate widget on your site,
    • package calculations such as dimensional weight.
    The label sender address adds the street lines from the Labeling tab. Return labels use the same address as the recipient.
